Running twice-yearly user immersions with leadership, which shaped the quarterly roadmap

Rush (by Hike)

Rush (by Hike) is a gaming app with 5 crore+ players, who play games like Ludo, Call Break and Carrom. Most of them are from Tier 2 and 3 cities in India, many with low English proficiency and new to the internet: a very different world from the team building the app.

User Immersion was a large-scale ethnographic study the research team ran every six months. We took leadership and senior management into players' own context, to meet them where they live and play.

The problem

Immersion only works if the right players show up. Recruitment was outsourced, took 40–45 days, and players often backed out at the last minute.

And the findings had to be ready in time, and strong enough, to shape the next quarter's roadmap.

How I approached it

Each round took about two months of preparation, in five stages. The process evolved with every round.

Two-step verification for recruitment

  1. Introductory callThe recruiter gauges interest and pairs players by location
  2. Confirmation callThe recruiter confirms details and profile screenshots
  3. Trust-building visitAn admin team member visits, so the player doesn't back out
  4. Final visitThe same admin takes stakeholders to meet the player

Impact

−81%last-minute back-outs

40–45 → 14–15 daysto recruit for each round

  • Burning issues fixed fast: leadership saw problems first-hand, and a TDS cashback was launched right after the second immersion
  • A direct line into the roadmap: immersion ran just before planning week, so its report shaped each next quarter
  • Foundational knowledge of players: key journeys and personas were evaluated, and we uncovered how players think and behave
  • Empathy from the top down: it became one of the most awaited exercises at Hike, and raised UX maturity across the company

Reflection: research operations are research

The most important work happened before anyone left the office. Building trust with players before the visit is what made the visits happen at all, and a report timed for planning week is what turned what leaders saw into roadmap decisions.
