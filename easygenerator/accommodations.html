Case study 2 · Accommodations

Mixed-methods discovery research: understanding how special education is mandated, planned and delivered in US classrooms to lift Accommodations adoption by 8 points

Accommodations is how Wayground meets special education (SPED) requirements: a teacher sets a student's supports once, like extended time or read-aloud, and they apply automatically in every assignment and lesson.

Problem discovery

Adoption data showed anomalies we couldn't explain, e.g. homework got more accommodations than classwork, and some subjects disproportionately many.

A survey and 12 interviews with general education teachers answered those questions, but revealed something bigger: SPED teachers are central to accommodations from start to finish, and we didn't understand their world.

The problem

Accommodations was built from SPED plans, not from how SPED works on the ground: who's involved, who delivers accommodations and who's accountable. As our biggest business bet of the year, the team needed to understand this world, and it was my job to make that happen.

Reflection: my favorite part was getting to be the bridge between the team and the teachers

This study started with a few questions the data was posing, and we only wanted to understand why those things were happening. But once we opened the box, it became my biggest project at Wayground up to that point. We were able to solve a very key problem with the new Share access flow.

And more importantly: as a product researcher, a lot of my work is tied directly to developing a product or a feature. This project, however, was an excellent reminder of a crucial aspect of being a researcher. I was the bridge that connected users to the team building for them, bringing their worlds and their voices into the meeting rooms and into the day-to-day of my colleagues. It was one of the most rewarding things I did at work.

In that process, I got to build long-term professional relationships with special education teachers and facilitators, and got to know some of them, and their world, deeply. It was such a rewarding experience to learn from these brilliant, amazing people who truly care about what they do.
